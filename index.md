# Transportation Needs & Barriers

## Project background

*Identifying Travel Needs, Barriers, and Solutions* is a two-year transportation research project conducted at The University of Texas at Austin. This repository serves as the public-facing home for approved project reports and related research products.

The project examines how transportation systems shape people's ability to reach essential destinations, participate in daily activities, and maintain quality of life. The project brings together two complementary perspectives: the accessibility supplied by transportation and land-use systems, and the transportation conditions experienced by individuals, including travel barriers, transportation insecurity, perceived accessibility, activity participation, and well-being. Conventional transportation data document observed trips, travel modes, distances, and travel times well, but they do not necessarily show whether people can meet their needs, which activities they undertake less often, what transportation problems they experience, or how well available options support participation in daily life. This project responds to those gaps by combining place-based accessibility research with person-centered evidence about transportation needs and lived experience.

**Funding source.** The project was supported by the [National Center for Understanding Future Travel Behavior and Demand](https://tbd.ctr.utexas.edu/) (TBD), a U.S. Department of Transportation University Transportation Center.

**Research team.** **Md Hamidur Rahman** is a Ph.D. student and Graduate Research Assistant in the Community and Regional Planning Program, School of Architecture, at The University of Texas at Austin. **Alex Karner** is an Associate Professor in the Graduate Program in Community and Regional Planning, School of Architecture, at The University of Texas at Austin.


## Project objectives

The project was developed to:

- clarify relationships among transportation disadvantage, transport poverty, accessibility, unmet needs, and social exclusion;
- identify limitations in commonly used travel-survey measures;
- develop an original survey of transportation needs, barriers, and potential solutions;
- examine how spatial accessibility and perceived accessibility relate to activity participation and quality of life; and
- support data-driven and equitable transportation planning, measurement, and policy.

### Survey instrument

The survey instrument documents questions about transportation resources, travel behavior, recent barriers, transportation insecurity, perceived accessibility, unmet activity needs, well-being, and preferred transportation improvements ([Qualtrics survey instrument](deliverables/Qualtrics%20Survey%20Software.pdf)).
